### F-01 — The seal gate misses roughly a quarter of topic-breaking changes, while the same material instructs adopters to gate CI on it
- Topics: COR-04, COR-05.4, COR-06
- Evidence: COR-04's business section states the design's own measurement: per-symbol body hashes caught 0.76 of changes that made a topic wrong, versus 1.0 for per-file flags (`README.md:81`-`85`), and that the seal surface is "experimental and frozen, not the recommended way to keep a corpus true" (`docs/DESIGN.md:19`-`30`). - Failure: A team wires `sealmap verify` into CI as instructed. A behaviour change that alters a cited function through a path body hashes don't capture (the 24% class) passes the gate; a topic that is now wrong ships as "sealed and holding". Because the surface is frozen (`docs/DESIGN.md:19`-`30`), the gap cannot be closed without breaking the format.
- Confidence: high
- Severity: high
- Marked by authors: no (stated as a caution in prose; no Debt/Tension/Open marker, and the CI-gate recommendation stands uncorrected in COR-05)

### F-02 — The gate passes vacuously on the repository's own corpus, and any seal on a `path:line` topic can be silently removed
- Topics: COR-04.4, COR-06.2, COR-06.4, COR-05.4

### F-03 — An empty `--diff` with `-o` leaves yesterday's pack in place as today's answer
- Topics: COR-06.1, COR-06.4
- Evidence: When `--diff` selects nothing, the command exits 0 and writes nothing (`crates/sealmap/src/main.rs:712`-`714`); with `-o FILE`, "a pack from an earlier run is left in place, where a caller may read it as current". This sits against the business claim "The same request always produces the same file, so two reviewers... see the same thing" and "It never quietly drops content."
- Failure: A scheduled job runs `sealmap pack --diff main -o review.txt` nightly. On a no-change night the previous pack — built from a different selection and an older revision — is served to the reviewer or agent, who treats it as the current diff's evidence. The wrong topics get reviewed and the changed ones do not.
- Confidence: high
- Severity: medium
- Marked by authors: yes (Debt)

## Not judgeable from this material

1. **Concurrent writers.** Nothing states what happens when two `seal sign` runs or a `sign` and a `generate` write the lock or corpus directory simultaneously — whether there is any locking at all.
2. **Symlinks and races on the read/write paths.** `write` refuses symlinks and pre-checks every path (`contract.rs:160`-`199`), but nothing says the check and the write are atomic (a symlink planted in between), nor whether directory *reads* (`contract.rs:129`, `main.rs:482`-`487`) traverse symlinks.
3. **Secret and permission exposure.** Source windows in packs and exported git trees in `sealmap-since-*` temp directories could expose credentials in world-readable locations; permissions are never stated.
4. **How `--diff` "ignoring moves" identifies a moved symbol.** If `sym:` ids are path/module-derived, a move changes the id; the matching rule that makes a move a non-change (`pack.rs:357`-`370`) is not shown.
5. **Whether `sign` refuses a topic with zero citations**, i.e. whether an empty, always-holding seal entry can be created; and the scope of the 0.76-recall measurement (one dataset, one codebase) that underpins F-01.

# Post-mortem: sealmap evidence pipeline

**Incident class:** a review/approval decision with client-facing consequences was made on sealmap-generated evidence (a seal verdict, a corpus diagram, or a review pack) that was incomplete, stale, or weaker than the consumer believed. The three root causes below are mechanisms the corpus material itself documents; every claim carries the citation the material gives, and each can be re-checked at those lines. ### R-1 — The seal gate verifies less than it appears to: measured 0.76 recall, silent seal removal, and never-sealed legacy topics all pass
- Topics: COR-04, COR-04.4, COR-05, COR-06
- Chain of events:
  1. The operator adopts `sealmap verify` as the CI gate asserting "cited code unchanged" for trust-bearing topics.
  2. A behaviour change lands whose per-symbol body hashes do not change — the class the design's own measurement says it misses.
  3. Separately or additionally, a topic's lock entry and its `sealed:` pointer are removed together in the same commit, or the topic is a legacy `path:line` topic that was never sealed at all.
  4. `verify` exits 0 in all three cases.
  5. An agent or reviewer consults the now-false topic as fact and approves or makes a change on it; the wrong decision reaches the client.
- Evidence:
  - Per-symbol body hashes caught 0.76 of the changes that made a topic wrong, against 1.0 for per-file flags, while flagging only 1.19× fewer topics (`README.md:81`-`85`); the design says the seal surface is "experimental and frozen, not the recommended way to keep a corpus true" (`docs/DESIGN.md:19`-`30`).
  - Removing a topic's lock entry and its pointer together passes `verify` whenever the topic cites no `sym:` id, because it is then indistinguishable from one never sealed; "nothing remembers that a topic was once sealed" (`crates/sealmap-corpus/src/seal/check.rs:391`-`392`, COR-04.4 Debt).
  - Legacy `path:line` topics are coverage, not failures (`docs/DESIGN.md:161`-`163`).
  - The per-symbol rule "loses real changes"; `--diff` "is a convenience, not an answer" (`README.md:41`-`46`, COR-06 business).
- Warning signs visible today: the design freezes the seal surface while measuring a 24% miss rate; this repository's own corpus has no seals yet (COR-06.4); the silent-unseal debt is already filed.
- Confidence: high — every mechanism is stated; only the specific downstream decision is hypothesised.
- Severity: high

### R-2 — The diagrams agents are told to trust are silently lossy, and the corpus misdocuments its own check code
- Topics: COR-01, COR-02.1, COR-02.2, COR-02.5, COR-04.5
- Chain of events:
  1. A pipeline agent uses the generated corpus as its source of fact — the stated purpose: "what an agent reads when it needs the facts about a file without opening the code" (COR-01 business) and "the diagrams an agent actually reads" (COR-02 business).
  2. The function it consults makes more than `max_messages` calls (default 80), so every call past the budget is dropped from the diagram wherever it sits in the flow, with only a count (`crates/sealmap-corpus/src/lib.rs:162`, `crates/sealmap-corpus/src/sequence.rs:71`-`73`).
  3. Or the missed call is on a receiver of unknown type, which under the default external policy "is dropped before it reaches a diagram" (COR-02.2 Debt, `crates/sealmap-corpus/src/naming.rs:47`-`49`).
  4. Or the agent is reasoning about `classify` or `sign` themselves, whose generated diagrams omit the unparsable arm and the success arm respectively, because lowering drops any arm without a kept call — "read from the generated corpus alone, both functions look as if they had no such path" (`crates/sealmap-extract/src/lower.rs:71`, `crates/sealmap-corpus/src/seal/check.rs:146`, `crates/sealmap-corpus/src/seal/sign.rs:125`-`126`, COR-04.5 Debt).
  5. The agent's audit or modification is wrong in a way nobody downstream can see, and ships.
- Evidence: as cited above; additionally unresolved targets are left out of the crate graph entirely (`crates/sealmap-corpus/src/overview.rs:81`-`82`), and the business section states the complete call lists stay only in `_index.json`, not the diagrams (COR-02 business).
- Warning signs visible today: three author-filed debts state silent or count-only omission; the corpus's own safety-critical functions are the demonstrated victims.
- Confidence: high for the omission mechanisms; the harm chain through an agent decision is inferred.
- Severity: high

### R-3 — Review packs can serve stale or ambiguous evidence as current
- Topics: COR-06.1, COR-06.3, COR-06.4, COR-05.2
- Chain of events:
  1. Automation runs `sealmap pack --diff REV -o FILE` on a schedule to build review evidence.
  2. One run selects nothing (no topic changed); the command exits 0 and writes nothing (`crates/sealmap/src/main.rs:712`-`714`, COR-06.1 Debt).

  5. The header cannot disambiguate anyway when it does match: `+dirty` records only that tracked files differ from `HEAD`, "not how" — two packs of different uncommitted edits carry the same header (COR-06.1 Tension, `crates/sealmap/src/main.rs:769`-`770`); and untracked files never make it dirty (`crates/sealmap/src/main.rs:763`-`769`) even though the pack body is built from the working tree.
- Evidence: the `-o` staleness debt is stated verbatim (COR-06.1); the `+dirty` Tension is author-filed; the exit-0/no-write combination is stated at `main.rs:712`-`714`.
- Warning signs visible today: both the Debt and the Tension are already on record; no mechanism described clears or marks the output file on an empty selection.
- Confidence: high for the stale-file and `+dirty` mechanisms; the untracked-file body variant is inferred from the stated facts.
- Severity: high

### R-1 — Unresolvable and ambiguous calls are silently dropped from the call graph, so the agent acted on a graph missing live edges
- Topics: DEN-01.6, DEN-01.2
- Chain of events:
  1. A call in client code has an unknown or ambiguous receiver (dynamic dispatch, generic code, two reachable types with the same method name).
  2. The resolver produces `sym:? name` and, with two or more reachable candidates, "no edge is guessed" (`crates/sealmap-rust/src/resolve.rs:975-989`).
  3. The default external policy drops `sym:?` calls outright (DEN-01.6, traced-cause paragraph on the struct-literal defect).
  4. The dense tree shows the target callable with fewer or no callers, with no mark distinguishing "checked and external" from "failed to resolve".
  5. The agent treats the callable as unreferenced or its call path as nonexistent and removes or reroutes it; the real caller breaks in production.
- Evidence: The material states the drop policy directly ("which the default external policy drops") and the ambiguity rule directly (`crates/sealmap-rust/src/resolve.rs:975-989`, `crates/sealmap-rust/src/layout.rs:75`, `crates/sealmap-rust/src/layout.rs:247`). - Warning signs visible today: five defects of this exact class existed and were invisible to the prior corpus; `sym:?` calls leave no trace in the output; nothing in the material asserts a completeness check or an unresolved-call marker.
- Confidence: high that the mechanism exists as described; the link to this specific incident is inferred.

## extract / premortem

Post-mortem basis: the six extract topics at `verified_commit b9a6aebddd2e8379206eea6cfdd3ab84546724b4`. Every behavior cited below is stated in that material; where I reason past it, I say so. sealmap is a review-instrument vendor: its output is consumed as evidence by human reviewers and by seals. A data/custody/security incident therefore flows through one of three doors — the diagram omitted the harmful behaviour, the diagram showed the wrong target as certain, or the enforcement object (seal/fingerprint) detached from the code it was supposed to guard.

## Root causes, most likely first

### R-1 — Diagrams used as review evidence silently omit the calls that matter, while the calls they do show carry confidence markers
- Topics: EXT-04.3, EXT-04.4, EXT-03.4, EXT-03.6, EXT-05.4, EXT-03 (business)
- Chain of events: An engineer lands a change whose dangerous behaviour lives in a place the pipeline does not draw — a std call, a call on a receiver of unknown type, the body of a macro, a closure invoked through a variable, or the second call of a builder chain. The walker or resolver drops or displaces it; lowering prunes what is left; the diagram shows a benign flow whose remaining arrows are tagged `exact`. The reviewer approves against the diagram; the harmful path ships.
- Evidence: the default `NonStd` policy leaves std calls out, and "the cost of that density is that a reviewer never sees those calls" (EXT-04 business; `crates/sealmap-rust/src/lib.rs:70`-`74`: "std, prelude constructors and calls on receivers of unknown type are dropped so diagrams stay dense"; `crates/sealmap-extract/src/confidence.rs:51`). - Warning signs visible today: the material itself records these as Debt and still sells density as the feature; nothing in any topic marks an omission in the output.
- Confidence: high — every omission mechanism is stated; only the attribution to this incident is the counterfactual frame.
- Severity: high

### R-2 — A call is drawn with `exact` confidence to the wrong internal symbol, and the reviewer trusts the arrow
